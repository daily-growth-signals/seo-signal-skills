## The half-day keyword ritual

You know the afternoon. You spot a phrase—a customer line, a competitor’s new page, or a query that “looks hot”—and a hole opens in the calendar.

You pull search volume, KD, and CPC from Semrush. You screenshot Google Trends. You google the term and paste the top 10 domains and page types into a doc. Most of the time is not “analysis.” It is gathering scattered numbers and captures into one pile you can hand off.

Once the pile looks complete, you dump it into an AI chat: “Is this keyword worth doing?” Minutes later you get a long answer that sounds finished—and is hard to check. Which line maps to which screenshot? Which claim holds up? You tweak the prompt, add missing bits, and the afternoon disappears.

The steps are fine. The ritual is the problem: you spend the day consolidating data, then outsource the call to a model, while the evidence still lives in tabs and chat. When someone asks for the decision, it is still hard to point to one place.

## How to read the six signals

A high search volume or a soft difficulty score is not a decision by itself. The report organizes six questions so each signal stays in its lane.

**1. Search volume — how much demand is there?**  
Use Google Ads historical monthly search estimates and monthly history for the selected market and language to judge scale and consistency. Treat the figure as a demand estimate for that scope, not measured visits to your site. Volume can also differ from Ahrefs or Semrush because each model is different—prefer reading it next to Trends, SERP, and business fit in the same report.

**2. Google Trends — is interest rising or falling?**  
Read the relative 0–100 interest index over time. It shows direction and seasonality within the selected period; it is not a count of searches, and it is not absolute demand.

**3. Google results (SERP) — is there a ranking opening?**  
Inspect the observed pages, search intent, and where your target appears in that snapshot. Match the page type and intent you plan to serve against what already ranks.

**4. KD / backlink context — how strong is the competition?**  
Read difficulty together with available aggregate backlink context for the competitive set. Use it to sense how hard the SERP looks overall—not as a standalone green light.

**5. CPC — is there paid-search interest?**  
CPC is a bid signal for that market. It can show commercial heat; map it to commercial interest, not visitor value or customer lifetime value.

**6. Business fit — are these the users you serve?**  
Compare the search need with what your website or page can actually deliver. A keyword with solid demand and a soft SERP is still the wrong bet if you cannot fulfill the query honestly.

### Stance and action plan

When the evidence supports one, the report recommends a direction. The full set of stances looks like this:

- **Target** — demand, SERP, and fit align enough to invest now.
- **Start narrower** — begin with a more specific related term before the broad query.
- **Topic cluster** — cover the theme with a set of related pages, not one catch-all URL.
- **Improve existing** — strengthen a page you already have instead of publishing another.
- **Validation test** — run a lighter check before full content investment.
- **Defer** — wait; timing or evidence does not support spending capacity yet.
- **Do not prioritize** — keep it off the near-term roadmap.

Under the stance sits a three-part action plan: **what to do**, **why it matters** (tied to the signals), and **when to recheck** (a validation or stop condition). Read that plan as a checkable brief for the next work cycle—not as a ranking forecast.

## When to use this report

**Before creating a page.** Enter the keyword and the site or URL you plan to publish under. Confirm that demand, intent, and current results fit the page you intend to publish.

**When an existing page stalls.** Use that page’s URL as the target. Decide whether improving it is a clearer next step than starting over on a new URL.

**When a broad term looks tempting.** Inspect related terms and Trends together. Often the better move is a narrower first page—or a cluster—rather than one oversized target.

**When prioritizing content work.** Run separate reports for candidate keywords, then compare stances and action plans before you assign writers. The goal is a defensible queue, not a longer spreadsheet of volumes alone.
